# Worked example: the shape of a finished result

A composite illustration, not any one real person's actual voice guide, kept generic on purpose:
a voice guide built from real samples names things specific enough to be a little uncomfortable
to publish verbatim, which is exactly why it works for that one person and should never be reused
for someone else. This shows the structure to aim for.

---

Register: direct, first person, plain language over polished language. States a position, then
the reasoning, never the reverse.

Per-format rules, because one register does not fit every format:

- Status updates: shortest form that is still complete. Lead with what changed, not with process.
- Proposals and anything collaborative: state the position directly, then name what would change
  it. A flat, undiscussable claim reads as closed off in a format meant to invite pushback.
- Published opinion pieces: the flat, confident claim is the product, hedge less here than
  anywhere else.

Most often got wrong, checkable during a review pass:

1. Opening with context or scene-setting instead of the conclusion. First sentence should be the
   position or the concrete result.
2. Explaining only what happened, never why it matters or what the underlying mechanism is.
3. A uniform, unhedged tone in every format, including ones where the whole point is to invite
   disagreement.
4. Never naming a limit, a trade-off or a thing that did not work. One honest gap per piece is
   what makes the rest credible.
5. A specific punctuation or vocabulary ban this person actually asked for (their own words, not
   assumed).

What "not this voice" looks like for this person specifically: overly hedged, buries the point
three sentences in, or reads uniformly upbeat with no admitted limitation anywhere.

---

Notice what makes this usable: every rule is checkable against a specific piece of writing, not a
mood word like "authentic" or "punchy". Build the real version from the new user's own samples and
their own answer to "what does not-my-voice look like for you", never from this illustration.
